Validation

Besides unit tests of the individual operators, the test suite compares complete simulations with closed-form solutions from the MR literature (test/validation.jl). Each sequence is run long enough to reach its steady state, and the magnitude of the simulated transverse magnetization at the echo time is compared with the closed-form expression. The forward-sensitivity derivatives (simulate_derivatives_forward_sensitivity) are compared with the derivatives of the same expressions w.r.t. $T_1$, $T_2$ and $B_1$ (which scales the flip angle $\alpha$), so that they are checked independently of the simulator itself.

Below, $E_1 = e^{-TR/T_1}$ and $E_2 = e^{-TR/T_2}$, and the expressions give the transverse magnetization right after the RF pulse, per unit of $M_0$. The simulations are compared at the echo time, i.e. after an additional factor $e^{-TE/T_2}$.

The derivatives agree to within ~1e-7 (relative), which is the accuracy of the finite differences of the closed-form expressions they are compared with.

Spoiled gradient echo

With ideal spoiling of the transverse magnetization at the end of every TR, the steady state is given by the Ernst formula (Ernst & Anderson, 1966):

\[M_{xy} = \sin\alpha \frac{1 - E_1}{1 - E_1 \cos\alpha}.\]

Inversion recovery

A single excitation a time $TI$ after an ideal inversion gives

\[M_{xy} = \sin\alpha \left(1 - 2 e^{-TI/T_1}\right).\]

Unbalanced SSFP (FISP)

For a gradient-spoiled sequence without RF spoiling, in which the dephasing gradient dephases the magnetization completely within a voxel, the steady-state FID signal is (Hänicke & Vogel, 2003)

\[M_{xy} = \tan\frac{\alpha}{2} \left(1 - \frac{(E_1 - \cos\alpha)(1 - E_2^2)}{\sqrt{p^2 - q^2}}\right),\]

with $p = 1 - E_1\cos\alpha - E_2^2(E_1 - \cos\alpha)$ and $q = E_2(1 - E_1)(1 + \cos\alpha)$.

This is the limit of an infinite number of configuration states. With max_state states, the EPG simulation differs from it by a relative amount of the order of $E_2^{\text{max\_state}}$: for example ~3e-7 with 128 states at $TR = 10$ ms and $T_2 = 80$ ms. The test uses short enough $T_2$s (relative to TR) for this to be negligible.

Balanced SSFP

For a balanced sequence with alternating RF phase and off-resonance precession $\theta = 2\pi \, \Delta B_0 \, TR$ per TR (Freeman & Hill, 1971),

\[M_{xy} = \frac{(1 - E_1) \sin\alpha \sqrt{1 - 2E_2\cos\theta' + E_2^2}}{(1 - E_1\cos\alpha)(1 - E_2\cos\theta') - (E_1 - \cos\alpha)(E_2 - \cos\theta')E_2},\]

with $\theta' = \theta + \pi$ to account for the alternating RF phase. The echo time is $TR/2$. The test uses pSSFP3D with a single, instantaneous RF sample, and the same sequence written out as RF and gradient waveforms for Generic3D. The latter needs a finite RF duration; with a 1 ns block pulse, relaxation during the pulse causes the ~1e-8 difference.
